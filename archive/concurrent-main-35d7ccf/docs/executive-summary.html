Why pruning can help — and what it does not prove

The shared instrument uses a 300 m triangular kernel and Tversky weights α=0.2, β=0.8. Additional predictions can add false-positive mass without improving the maximum coverage of a withheld fault pixel. Sparse dots can therefore outperform a dense ridge. Removing low-value dots is a plausible explanation for the supplied score ladder, not a causal experiment on hidden organizer labels.

The precise marginal condition is ΔTP > DTI × (ΔTP + αΔFP + βΔFN). A fixed “credit per dot” threshold is not universally valid because coverage saturates and one dot may affect multiple truth cells. Hidden faults are unavailable; no legitimate calculation here establishes a new score above 0.2778 or either claimed leaderboard high.

This is a fault-mapping target relevant to geothermal exploration, not a validated geothermal-vent detector. Geological confounders and external geothermal validation must be tracked separately.
