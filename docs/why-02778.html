Why did GEMSDOE32 report 0.2778?

Short answer: the most plausible mechanism is sparse-emission efficiency, but the available evidence does not prove that mechanism caused the reported value. The number is OWNER-REPORTED, not organizer-confirmed in the reviewed evidence; no receipt binds it to the exact TIFF hash.

What is directly verified about the file

  • The immutable historical TIFF at the inspected GitHub commit has SHA-256 c55bafc470054e8271dcb89347a17e07fefe50de6af6e6ba6c4b169ef7ab6fa9.
  • Its stored raster is single-band float32, EPSG:32611, 3730 × 3292, with affine transform (100, 0, 243350, 0, -100, 4508550); all stored cells are finite in [0,1]. It has 37,654 positive cells.
  • Its positive mask is an exact subset of the selected GEMSDOE25 D2.8 positive mask: D2.8 contains 44,090 positive cells and 6,436 of them are absent from H33-B2. This is independently computed raster lineage, not a score explanation.
  • The H33-B2 mask is not unique under the requested final-dot rule: 100% of its positive pixels are within 3 pixels of the audited GEMSDOE24 winner, above the 70% limit.

These are byte-level properties. None reveals whether a dot landed near an unseen expert-mapped fault, what the hidden test truth contains, or how the organizer's private score components were distributed.

Why thinning can help under a distance-weighted Tversky objective

The competition description and the shared research implementation specify a distance-weighted Tversky form. With prediction mass P, hidden truth set G, weighted true-positive credit TP, false-positive mass FP, and false-negative mass FN, the index is:

DTI = TP / (TP + α·FP + β·FN)
α = 0.2, β = 0.8
k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0), with R = 300 m

Under the triangular kernel, a prediction near a hidden trace can earn partial credit. A thick, adjacent ribbon can therefore pay repeatedly for overlapping neighborhoods while barely increasing new coverage. Removing one such redundant pixel may reduce false-positive mass with almost no loss of credited truth; conversely, removing a unique bridge pixel can reduce recall. The right quantity is the marginal change in TP, FP, and FN—not dot count alone. The exact general condition for a change to improve the ratio is:

ΔTP · D > TP · (ΔTP + α·ΔFP + β·ΔFN)
where D = TP + α·FP + β·FN

This explains how a sparse descendant could rank better than a denser parent if it preferentially deletes low-credit redundant mass. It does not show that this happened for the unseen labels. The hidden test truth and official TP/FP/FN accounting are not available in this checkout.

Can a new unique submission beat it?

In principle, yes: a genuinely different detector could find hidden fault segments that the historical masks missed, and a well-calibrated emission could reduce false-positive mass. The available evidence does not support predicting that it will beat the reported H33-B2 result. The immediate decision is therefore not to submit, not because improvement is impossible, but because no candidate can be validated without the missing source rasters, cached features, shared holdout, explicit lane, and a valid score receipt.
