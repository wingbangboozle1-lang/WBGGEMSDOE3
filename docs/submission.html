03 / Why 0.2778, and can we beat it?

A metric-level explanation

The objective is not ordinary accuracy

The official distance-weighted Tversky index is DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw), with k(d)=max(1−d/3,0). TP uses a max over predictions, so a single well-placed unit dot can cover many truth pixels within 300 m, while redundant dots add FP at 0.2 each. The organizer has confirmed that known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels are masked from evaluation and that the score is pooled over all public-test pixels.

Therefore the optimum is a sparse, well-placed coverage set

A dotted lattice along a high-susceptibility network harvests near-full kernel credit with few FPs. H33-B2's measured difference from H27-4 is exactly the removal of 2,545 dots within 2 px of mapped faults — pure FP reduction in the masked/near-masked zone. That explains the highest owner-reported claim across the nine sites, but the claim is not a receipt and the same trick cannot be repeated: a renamed or re-encoded copy is a duplicate.

Our improvement is a different estimator and a different placement

SGCE estimates a hidden-fault density from 20 physical fields plus geometric continuations of visible faults, then lets the exact DTI marginal gain decide how many dots to emit and where. On the identical 8-cell, whole-segment, 1.5 km-collar holdout it reaches 0.1675 (95% CI 0.1524–0.1820) vs 0.0992, paired Δ +0.0683 with CI entirely above zero. The leakage canary's worst single field was GEOM_d_vis_neg at AUC 0.748 (<0.90).

Can we guarantee a higher leaderboard score?

No. The holdout hides catalogue segments; the scored population is expert-labelled new faults, whose generating process the organizers decline to disclose. The public leaderboard has moved to 0.4544. Our projection is a calibrated guess, not a score. What we can guarantee is a new, unique, format-valid file that beats the best local comparator on a matched, leak-checked holdout — the only defensible pre-upload evidence available.

04 / Irregularities flagged for review

Known limits, not hidden caveats

  • The literal positive-support registry test is unsatisfiable because of a registered dense continuous raster; the content-aware interpretation used here is preregistered and needs ratification (preregistration).
  • Historical “scores” are owner reports; no submission-page receipt ties any of them to uploaded bytes. The leaderboard is a timestamped public observation, not our result.
  • Competition rasters are hash-pinned public mirrors, not organizer-authenticated downloads. Band semantics (notably tc) are not independently verified against official metadata.
  • The organizer's “null/NaN outside bounds” wording conflicts with an all-finite writer; two encodings are supplied. Portal acceptance has not been tested.
  • The Final Prize Round expands labels by expert review of all submissions; a locally lower-scoring but geologically novel prediction could be re-scored differently. Organizers explicitly declined to describe the expert labelling sources.
  • Parallel lanes are separate experiments. HCE-1, CSPES, RFL and TDE30k published their own pages under docs/hce/, docs/cspes/ and their own JSON cards. Their "SUBMIT-WITH-CAVEAT" or negative gates refer to different rasters, not to this SGCE file. Those artifacts are research-only; the decision and download on this page are the repository-wide recommendation.
