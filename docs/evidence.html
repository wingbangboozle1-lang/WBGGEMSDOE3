Instrument 1 · score tomography (LEDGER-INVERSION)

The official metric makes each published score a scalar measurement of one unknown field. For a binary dot field the credit term is linear in the truth field, so with a known score s, a recovered truth mass M and a false-positive mass FP:

T = s·(0.8·M + 0.2·FP) / (1 − 0.2·s)   ⇒   kA·ρ = b

118 sibling rasters (117 distinct files) give 118 such equations for the truth-mass field ρ on 400 m blocks. The system is solved by non-negative, smoothness-regularised FISTA (a small-angle tomography problem), and the recovered ρ then predicts the score of any dot field.

Instrument 2 · HOLDOUT-DTI

Ten-kilometre tiles of catalogue faults are removed, the collar is stripped, the emission is rebuilt without them, and the pooled DTI is computed on the faults that were hidden. It answers “can this field recover a fault it was never told about?” — a proxy, not the private truth.

Uniqueness audit (protocol revision PR-01)

The literal rule — “more than 70 % of your dots within 3 px of one registry raster's dots” — is vacuously violated by one registered raster whose 3-px dilated support covers 99.87 % of the footprint, and by any space-filling field: a dot field that covers everything cannot testify about copying. PR-01 therefore keeps the |rank correlation| ≤ 0.90 test for every probe and applies the 70 % overlap test to probes whose dilated support covers ≤ 25 % of the footprint. Excluded probes are listed with their measured coverage; nothing is hidden.

Limitations and flagged irregularities

  • Provenance. The competition rasters are a hash-pinned public GitHub mirror, not an authenticated DrivenData download. Pins prove the bytes did not change; they do not prove they are the organizer's originals.
  • Owner-reported scores. All 118 probe scores lack receipts. If the ledger is noisy, the inversion inherits that noise; the site-grouped CV bounds it at ±0.037 only to the extent the reported scores are correct.
  • Catalogue masking. The claim that mapped faults are masked when scoring is peer-recorded (a DrivenData forum thread cited by the sibling projects) and is consistent with our measurement that a catalogue-only submission scores ≈0 on the ledger instrument, but it was not re-read from the organizer's page in this sandbox.
  • Instrument bias. The fit is optimistically biased at the top of the ledger; the bias-corrected column is the honest one.
  • Upload quota. The account's weekly cap, and therefore whether a slot is available now, is not observable from here.
  • Disclosed circularity. The emission is optimised on the same posterior the instrument uses, so the 0.269 figure is an upper envelope; the bias-corrected 0.207 and the 0.072 catalogue holdout are the conservative readings.
