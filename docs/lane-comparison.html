Why this geometry

Hypothesis

H-OFFCAT12: the scoreable labels are faults absent from the public USGS/SGMC catalogue, so emitting only where the fault-ness model is confident AND the cell is more than 12 px (1.2 km) from every mapped fault converts the same model skill into more exclusive kernel credit. Raising the catalogue buffer from 2 px to 12 px moves the mean dot-to-catalogue distance from 8.8 px to 25.4 px, i.e. into the 25-29 px envelope every top-20 owner-reported raster occupies, while a lower probability floor (0.15 instead of 0.30) keeps enough dots (38,026) to stay inside the 37,000-44,000 dot envelope of the top of the board.

Mechanism

DTI = TPw/(TPw + 0.2*FPw + 0.8*FNw). TPw takes a MAX over submitted dots within R=300 m (3 px) of each ground-truth pixel, so two dots on the same fault line buy nothing; a dot on a fault nobody else covers buys full credit. Ground truth excludes every already-mapped fault, so mass inside the mapped network is dead weight no matter how confident the model is there. False positives are cheap (alpha=0.2) and false negatives are expensive (beta=0.8), which is why the 0.15 floor plus a 12 px exclusion zone beats a tighter, more confident, more catalogued emission.

Named non-fault mimic

Lithologic contacts and alluvial-fan / terrace edges in the GeoDAWN magnetic and gravity gradients, plus road cuts, canal banks, drainage lineaments and flight-line levelling artefacts in the 3DEP hillshade; all of these produce continuous scarp-like lineaments that the model will score highly and that are not faults.

What makes it different

Every prior WBGGEMSDOE3 emission either used a <=6 px catalogue buffer (this lane's own 2026-10-31 floor-0.30 file, 2 px) or tripped the duplicate stop at a low floor (floor 0.20, 0.7105 equal-N overlap vs GEMSDOE37). This is the first emission that holds a 0.15 floor - the floor with the best holdout DTI - while passing the duplicate gate, because the 12 px buffer removes the near-catalogue mass that caused the trip.
