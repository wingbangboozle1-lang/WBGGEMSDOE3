Why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778 — and whether higher is possible

Evidence classes used on this page. OWNER-REPORTED = a score written down by a sibling repo (no DrivenData receipt is available to us). OBSERVED-PUBLIC-PAGE = read from drivendata.org today. COMPUTED-HERE = recomputed in this repo from bytes we hash-checked. HOLDOUT-DTI = our spatially-blocked evaluator. Nothing here is an ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED score of a file we produced.

1. The metric, algebraically

Official definitions (metric page): kernel k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0); TPw = Σ_g max_x p(x)k(d(x,g)); FPw = Σ_x p(x)[1 − max_g k(d(x,g))]; FNw = Σ_g [1 − max_x p(x)k]. With a binary emission of N dots and |G| truth pixels, let M = Σ_x p(x) max_g k. Then FPw = N − M, FNw = |G| − TPw and

DTI = TPw / [ 0.2 (TPw + N − M) + 0.8 |G| ].

Two asymmetries drive everything: credit is a max (a second dot beside an already-credited truth pixel adds nothing) but false-positive mass is a sum (every extra dot costs 0.2 unless it sits near truth). Because the 3-px kernel pays partial credit out to 300 m, a sparse dotted lattice keeps almost all credit of a solid line while paying a fraction of the N term.

Break-even rule (COMPUTED-HERE). Adding one isolated dot changes DTI by ≥ 0 iff its marginal credit exceeds about 0.2·DTI. At DTI = 0.2778 that is 0.056 kernel-credit units; with Σk = 9.38 over the 25-offset (radius-3-pixel) kernel support this means the hidden-truth density around the dot must exceed ≈ 0.006 per 100 m pixel — several times the footprint-wide average (≈ 0.001–0.002 for a hidden-truth mass of 4–10 thousand pixels, the bootstrap range found here). A dot below that density lowers the score.

Each row is a deletion or an addition that crosses the break-even density, which is why the improvements are small and monotone — the champion is a locally optimal pruning of one network, not a new source of information.

3. What the ledger does and does not show (COMPUTED-HERE)

  • 112 distinct live-scored rasters were hash-verified and re-measured. Independent rasters that share only 51–67 % of their dots with H19-5 still score 0.219–0.248 (e.g. 62 % → 0.2478). So hidden truth is not confined to the H19-5 network.
  • Rank correlation between score and "fraction of dots near H19-5" is 0.70, but all top rasters descend from H19-5, so this is confounded; a claim such as "73–96 % of truth lies on the network" is not identified by the ledger.
  • With the product stratum (catalogue distance × H19-5 distance) a ledger-calibrated model predicts held-out sites with MAE 0.026 (leave-site-out), 90th-percentile error 0.065. None of the 66 independent fields (19 bands, lidar, radiometrics, GeoDAWN, derived gradients, distances) improves on the stratum-only baseline in nested CV. The leakage canary passes: the largest single-field AUC against the catalogue is 0.658 (< 0.90).

4. Is a higher score possible?

Yes — the public board already shows it (leaderboard, 2026-10-26): #1 is 0.4437,

5 (the initial-round prize line) is 0.3561, and #50 is 0.2826, so 0.2778 would sit just outside the top 50.

Even with zero false positives, DTI = c/(0.2c + 0.8) for coverage c = TPw/|G|; 0.2778 needs c ≥ 0.235, 0.3561 needs c ≥ 0.307 and 0.4437 needs c ≥ 0.39. The leaders therefore recover at least ~40 % of the hidden truth with few false positives. We found nothing in the nine sibling repos, and no signal in the 66 fields examined here, that carries that much ledger-identified information. Our opinion (not a result): with the mirrored data alone the family is near its ceiling; a step change needs new information such as metre-scale scarps (3DEP 1 m DEM), InSAR, or newer fault mapping — all untested because they could not be fetched in this sandbox.
