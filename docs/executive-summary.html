5. Why 0.2778 happened, and the ceiling argument

QuestionAnswer
Why did the best sibling file get its score?`gemsdoe32-h33-2-b2…-zeros` (0.2778, owner-reported) has 37,654 dots whose support is an exact subset of a GEMSDOE28 base after 2,545 cells were pruned near a mirrored catalogue. Under DTI's α=0.2 false-positive weight, deleting zero-credit dots cannot lower the score and raises it whenever the removed cells earned no kernel credit. That is a metric mechanism, not proof of cause: no organizer receipt ties 0.2778 to those bytes.
Can we beat 0.2778?Not by pruning. FP weight is α=0.2 but FN weight is β=0.8, so the binding term is coverage of the hidden set: with the fitted |G|≈— cells the ceiling for a 37k-dot sparse submission is ≈0.45, and the leaderboard leader is at 0.4544. Raising the score means finding true off-catalogue traces the scarp-ridge families cannot see — which is what the GGO lane targets (buried, morphologically weak, geophysically strong).
What is unique about our file?Selection is conditioned on morphological *weakness* under thick cover, which is the opposite sign of every sibling lane in the registry; the census measures the resulting overlap instead of asserting it.
