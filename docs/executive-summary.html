Why GEMSDOE32 reported 0.2778 — and whether more is achievable

What its file is. GEMSDOE32's published raster is a dense set of dots on and immediately beside the mapped catalogue traces, with a byte-level relationship to GEMSDOE28's H27-4 record (a strict subset with 2,545 removed pixels).

Why it scores 0.2778. The DTI pays for proximity, not identity: a truth pixel collects the best credit of any prediction within 3 px, so a dense trace gives high recall for the majority of withheld faults that sit close to mapped ones, while the β-weighted FP term is spread thin over a large dot count. Pruning the low-credit dots can raise the ratio — which is a plausible *arithmetic* account of why a subset of another file scored higher, not a verified causal explanation of the hidden labels.

Cross-check from a sibling lane. A ledger-inversion estimator fitted on the nine owner-reported scores reproduces GEMSDOE32 at 0.3273 from its dot geometry alone (owner report 0.2778) — see focus_estimates_2026_10_31.json. That is an ESTIMATE, not a receipt, but it supports the reading that 0.2778 is mostly explained by hit-rate and dot count rather than by hidden-label luck.

Is higher achievable? Yes in principle, and this lane is the evidence for how: the holdout shows withheld faults land 3–12 px off the visible traces, which trace-followers waste. Our band geometry beats the best trace-following registry file on every fold by +0.011 to +0.057 paired DTI. Whether that transfers to the platform is unproven: on these folds the nine focus rasters score ≤0.105 while their owners report 0.24–0.28, so the holdout systematically under-reads platform scores.

What would have to be true for the top of the board

The leader sits at 0.4544. Closing that gap needs recall at 3 px plus a low FP mass, and the remaining levers in order of expected value are: (1) a segment-level, not pixel-level, corridor prior that predicts where a new strand starts; (2) a spacing rule tuned to the truth density, since 4 px currently over-covers; (3) a second, independent emission pass on a complementary band. Each is a 10–30 minute experiment on the existing feature stack and holdout harness.
