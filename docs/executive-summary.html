The metric, and why the champion's recipe worked

For a binary dot field of n dots, with M hidden-truth cells,

T = Σg∈G maxx∈P k(d(x,g)),  FPw = Σx∈P k(dist(x,G)),  DTI = T / (T + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·(M−T))

Adding one dot changes DTI by ≥ 0 only if its marginal credit exceeds ≈ 0.2·DTI. At DTI ≈ 0.28 that bar is ≈ 0.056 kernel-credit units, i.e. a local hidden-truth fraction of roughly 0.6 % inside the 3-pixel kernel footprint. Everything in the champion family is a consequence of that threshold: thinning removes dots below it, and the catalogue-annulus deletions remove dots inside the masked band where credit is impossible. It is a pruning family, so it has no remaining source of new information — which is exactly why the score saturates near 0.28.
