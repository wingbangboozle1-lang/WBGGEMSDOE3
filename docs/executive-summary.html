Geological hypothesis and mechanism (H66-SPRP)

Hypothesis: the official 19-band GeoDAWN stack carries a fault signature that generalises to faults that are not in the public USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. A per-cell classifier trained on the visible catalogue, using only catalogue-independent transforms of that stack, recovers withheld catalogue segments far above chance; the resulting probability field is converted into a sparse emission under the exact DTI bar.

Physical signature targeted: local standardised contrast (the template's z transform, a 9-cell local anomaly detector) and global quantile rank of every magnetic, gravity, elevation, geodetic, conductivity and heat-flow band — i.e. the edge/lineament expression of a fault in magnetics and gravity, not a catalogue-distance cue.

Why it can catch a fault that is missing from the catalogue: the classifier never sees where the catalogue is. It learns the geophysical expression of a fault from mapped faults only, and is then asked for that expression everywhere, including areas with little or no mapping.

Named non-fault mimic: lithologic and weathering contacts, buried basin-margin flexures, and magnetic-source edges (mafic intrusions) that mimic a fault's local contrast. These are the classic false positives of magnetics/gravity lineament mapping and are the reason the canary, the holdout and the DTI-bar allocation are all reported.

How it differs from the nine focus files: those nine are one and the same network (the h19-5 dotted skeleton) re-thinned and re-pruned; this file is built from a supervised probability field over the official stack with no catalogue-derived feature at all.

Leakage canary: max single-feature AUC 0.6465 across 38 features and 4 folds (threshold 0.90) — no feature carries withheld-label information.

GEMSDOE32 · reported 0.2778

Why that file scores highest, and whether it can be beaten

Reported result

OWNER-REPORTED 0.2778; there is no verified organizer receipt. The score itself has not been independently reproduced from portal-visible labels.

Observed construction (byte-verified)

GEMSDOE32's 37,654 positive cells are an exact subset of GEMSDOE28's 40,199-cell base: the rule “base AND distance > 2.0 px from the mapped catalogue” reproduces the file with 0 mismatches over the 12.28 M-cell grid. GEMSDOE31's dot set is identical to that base. So the reported pair GEMSDOE31 (0.2708) → GEMSDOE32 (0.2778) differs only by that exclusion.

Mechanism

The organiser masks known-fault pixels out of scoring, but the reply does not mask neighbouring pixels. A dot 1.4–2 px from a mapped fault therefore keeps its whole false-positive weight while earning little true-positive credit; deleting that band lowers the denominator's FP term. The marginal algebra of the metric agrees: adding one dot needs kernel credit k > α·DTI ≈ 0.056 to break even, and every marginal addition tested in that family hurt.

Can it be beaten?

Not by re-cutting that network: every dot of the 121,131-cell parent network lies within 3 px of the family, so a “different” variant of it is a duplicate. Beating it needs dots on hidden truth the family does not cover, i.e. new geological information — which is what this lane attempts with a catalogue-independent detector and a DTI-optimal emission. We cannot verify a leaderboard score from here: this mechanism is a byte-verified construction that is consistent with the metric, but it is not a verified causal account of a score we cannot see. What we publish is the holdout instrument reading and a calibrated estimate, both labelled as such.
