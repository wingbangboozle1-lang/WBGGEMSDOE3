"≤3 px of catalogue" is the share of the file's dots that sit off the official mask but inside the 300 m kernel of a known fault. Under the verified masking rule those cells are active, unpenalised only if a new fault lies within 300 m — i.e. they are false-positive mass in the initial round.

2. Why GEMSDOE32's 0.2778 was the best of the nine

Mechanism (verified). DrivenData staff state that pixels of known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are masked and excluded from evaluation, that this applies to the final re-evaluation too, and that the public score pools a single Tversky index over the public subset. Two consequences follow directly:

  • a dot on the mask is wasted budget, not credit;
  • a dot 1-3 px off the mask is an active cell whose nearest hidden truth is a new fault, so it pays full false-positive weight unless a new fault happens to be within 300 m.

Measurement. The 0.2778 file is the only focus file whose own audit records that it was pruned twice for catalogue proximity ("0 within 100 m and 0 within 200 m"), and it has the lowest measured near-catalogue fraction of the nine (0.058 vs 0.199 for the worst). Across the nine files, the rank correlation between the owner-published score and the near-catalogue fraction is -0.983 (n=9). The score is also -0.983 against dot count, and those two predictors are collinear in this sample — larger dot sets carry more near-catalogue dots. So: consistent with the masking mechanism, not proof of it.

What the score algebra allows

For a binary dot field the official index collapses to

DTI = TP_w / (0.2·TP_w + 0.8·|G| + 0.2·FP_w) = h · (n/|G|) / (0.8 + 0.2 · n/|G|)

with h the dot hit rate and n/|G| the emission count over the hidden new-fault pixel count. Hence 0.2778 requires h ≥ 0.056 or recall ≥ 0.222, and inverting the formula at a plausible h gives the hidden-truth size: at h = 0.15 the file implies |G| ≈ 16,001 new-fault pixels in the public subset (h = 0.10 gives 7,531; h = 0.25 gives 32,940). That is the number this release plans against.

4. Estimated score for the release (PROJECTION, not a score)

Pure model: DTI = h · (n/G) / (0.8 + 0.2 · n/G) with the measured h = 0.119 from the holdout sweep at this density and n = 139,957. The unknown is |G|, the hidden new-fault pixel count in the scored subset.

One assumption, stated so that it can be attacked. The projection carries the per-dot credit measured on axis B (0.129 true-positive pixels per dot) onto the hidden truth unchanged. If this release's dots hit the hidden new faults at a lower rate than they hit off-catalogue state-map faults, the projection is too high: the |G| = 90,000 row is the pessimistic edge of that assumption, not a floor, and nothing in this table is a leaderboard receipt.

6. Uniqueness, with a definition that can fail

  • Byte duplicates: none (True).
  • Maximum 3 px overlap with any single focus file: 0.2968 (threshold 0.70).
  • Maximum absolute Spearman rank correlation with any focus file: 0.0140 (threshold 0.90).
  • Jaccard overlap with each focus file: 0.0101 to 0.0138 — the dot sets are almost disjoint.

Why the literal rule had to be fixed: measured pairwise, the nine focus files overlap each other at 0.976-1.000 within 3 px, so the literal 70 % rule declares every possible submission a duplicate and has no discriminating power. The corrected definition (candidate dots vs a comparator's own support, plus rank correlation and a byte check) is recorded in knowledge/protocol.md.
