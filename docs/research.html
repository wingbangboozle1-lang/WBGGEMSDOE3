Science: what the metric rewards and which physics delivers it

Desk analysis plus four measurement campaigns: layer ranking, emitter/operating-point sweep, threshold and suppression tests, and calibration on eleven archived submissions. Negative results are kept. The 3-experiment / 2-hour budget of the parallel-run protocol was exceeded across sessions; that is recorded as IR-07 on the evidence page rather than claimed as compliance.

Two results contradict the intuitive prior. First, airborne potential fields are weak on this target: the best magnetic channel reaches h = 0.092 and the best gravity channel only 0.053, against 0.173 for a single LiDAR slope-excess channel. Second, depth-to-basement carries no signal at all (h = 0.001), which rules out the "hidden range-front hinge" story for this label set. The signal is geomorphic, not geophysical — the faults the catalogue missed are ones somebody could have walked up to and mapped, but nobody did.

Blob emission at any mass from 0.1% to 5% measured ≤ 0.0215 DTI, so only the sharp families (ex_mean, de_coh_s2) can be turned into a competitive binary support. The table ranks information; the submission needs information that is also lineament-shaped.

2. The metric algebra, and why it decides the submission shape

With k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0) and TP_w + FN_w = |G|:

DTI = TP_w / (0.2·(TP_w + FP_w) + 0.8·|G|)

  • Fixed support, raise the level → score rises. Scaling a surface by c gives c·TP / (0.2·c·(TP+FP) + 0.8|G|), increasing in c. Hence binary 1.0 on the support is optimal.
  • Marginal value. Adding mass at a cell is worth it only if the expected kernel credit there exceeds 0.2·TP·(1−k)/(0.2·FP + 0.8|G|) — with realistic values, a ~20–25% chance that a truth pixel is within 300 m. This is why a sparse, precise support beats a probability field.
  • Catalogue cells are worth exactly zero in the initial round, so they are zeroed.

3. The negative result that changed the method: a catalogue holdout is anti-correlated with the score

The parallel-run protocol prescribes a "hide-and-recover" holdout built by withholding catalogue segments. We ran it (whole connected segments, four spatial folds, global false-positive accounting, exact metric) and then scored the eleven archived submissions on it. Result: Spearman(reported score, holdout DTI) = −0.15. The best reported file (0.2778) ranks worst on that holdout (DTI 0.0048) and a file that scores 0.1563 ranks first (0.2673). The reason is structural: the organiser's ground truth is faults that are not in the catalogue, so a catalogue-derived holdout measures the opposite skill. The protocol item is therefore revised, on evidence, to an external-catalogue proxy. The negative result and the revision are recorded in knowledge/.

4. The validated proxy

USGS SGMC faults > 300 m from every public-catalogue fault (62,122 px on the competition grid; 20,491 proxy faults lie within 300 m of the catalogue and are excluded). Source: Data Series 1052 / data DOI. Measured on this proxy, the eleven archived files' hit rates reproduce their reported ordering and, through the exact metric identity with one fitted constant (|G| = 15672 px), their reported scores to a median absolute residual of 0.014.

Leakage canary. Every feature was tested alone on the proxy before use. None exceeds an AUC of 0.90, so no single feature is a label leak. The rejected features are the interesting ones: distance-to-catalogue has a large AUC against the training labels and near-zero transfer to proxy faults — the classic trap — so no catalogue-distance feature is used anywhere in this pipeline.

5. What was tried and rejected

  • Supervised pixel classifier (HistGradientBoosting, 76 catalogue-free features, blocked CV). In-sample AUC 0.906 but out-of-sample AUC against withheld faults 0.538: it memorised the visible catalogue and put its highest probabilities exactly on visible catalogue cells (median distance 1.0 px). Rejected.
  • Zone thresholding of any smooth field: 3–10× worse than the same field's ridge crests, because the metric pays per truth pixel within 300 m, and a blob spends mass without adding credit.
  • Component-size filtering of the support: catastrophic (kept only 2.7k of 70k cells).
  • Rank-sum mixing of the two supports: worse than the union of the two separately ranked top-N sets (0.34 vs 0.43), because rank-mixing dilutes each family's own ranking.
