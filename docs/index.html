02 / The stop, explained

A rule conflict, proven from the rasters

One reference covers the entire footprint.

Under the shared template, a “dot” is any finite value greater than zero. A registered 17GEMSDOE probability raster is positive at all 5,167,373 of 5,167,373 allowed cells. Every nonempty in-footprint prediction therefore overlaps it at distance zero: 100%, exceeding the requested 70% stop.

This is a proof about the literal definition, not a claim that every probability pixel is geologically a dot. No exemption was silently introduced.

03 / Understand the incumbent

Why did the B2 family do well?

What the files show

GEMSDOE32’s B2 raster is an exact in-footprint subset of a GEMSDOE28 H27-4 base: 37,654 dots retained from 40,199, with 2,545 removed near mapped faults. This is a decoded-pixel measurement, not a verified explanation of a score.

Under the official 300 m triangular kernel, nearby predictions can cover fault pixels, while redundant or distant predictions can add false-positive cost. The low false-positive weight may favor broad recovery. Hidden labels and authenticated receipts are unavailable, so the causal explanation remains a hypothesis.

Can a new method beat it?

Unknown. No matched holdout result or calibrated leaderboard forecast was produced here. Renaming, changing nodata encoding, or perturbing the old raster would not prove uniqueness or scientific improvement.

The next proposed lane tests cross-scale agreement of magnetic and gravity edges, with lithologic contacts and survey stripes as explicit non-fault mimics. It remains untested behind the stop.
