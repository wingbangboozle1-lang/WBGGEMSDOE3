The answer to “why did 0.2778 win, and can we beat it?”

Why it won. The metric is DTI = T / (T + 0.2·FP + 0.8·(|G|−T)) with a 300 m triangular kernel. Credit is a maximum over dots, so extra dots beside an already-credited fault pixel add nothing, while false-positive mass is a sum, so every useless dot costs 0.2. The H33-B2 family therefore wins by deleting dots, not by adding information: 121,131 solid dots (0.1922) → 44,090 dotted (0.2600) → 40,199 with the catalogue 100 m annulus dropped (0.2708) → 37,654 with the 100–200 m annulus also dropped (0.2778). Each step removes pure cost.

Can we beat it? Not with a genuinely new field under the evidence available here. Our instrument says a novel official-data field reaches 0.269 (raw) / 0.207 (bias-corrected) and the ledger's own 118 files show that only descendants of the H19-5 network exceed 0.25. A higher score would require either new information (metre-scale scarps were tested and do not lift the estimate) or re-uploading the incumbent network, which is a duplicate.
